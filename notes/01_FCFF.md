# Lecture 1 — Free Cash Flows to the Firm (AKA Cash Flow from Assets)

## Framing visuals
- Finance cycle: Financiers provide capital → Firm procures assets → Assets produce goods/services → Revenue → Net income → Excess distributed to financiers (or plowback). FCFF = the "excess distributed" node.
- Finance tree: Procurement / Deployment / Distribution → operational efficiency, go/no-go decisions, asset purchases/sales (valuation).
- Value = Sum of PV of expected future CASH FLOWS.

## Core formula (asset side)
FCF = OCF − NCS − ΔNWC
- OCF = EBIT + Depreciation − Taxes
- NCS = End NFA − Beg NFA + Depreciation
- ΔNWC = (End CA − End CL) − (Beg CA − Beg CL)
- Compact: FCF = EBIT(1−T) + Dep − NCS − ΔNWC

## Worked example
EBIT 1,014; Dep 500; Taxes 368; End NFA 2,194; Beg NFA 2,261; End CA 3,625; End CL 1,787; Beg CA 3,596; Beg CL 2,140
OCF 1,146; NCS 433; ΔNWC 382; FCF 331

## Financing side check
FCF = CF to creditors + CF to shareholders
- CF creditors = Debt paydown (Beg LTD − End LTD) + Interest
- CF shareholders = Stock repurchases (Beg CS&APIC − End CS&APIC) + Dividends
Example: Beg LTD 581, End 538, Interest 93 → 136; Beg CS 372, End 462, Div 285 → 195; total 331

## Preemptive questions (instructor's emphasis)
1. Why add depreciation in NCS? Beg NFA 10M + CapEx 1M − Dep 0.5M = End 10.5M → CapEx = End − Beg + Dep.
2. Why EBIT not EBT? Interest is a payment to financiers; FCFF must include ALL cash to financiers. Deducting interest removes it from FCF.
3. Taxes on EBIT or EBT? EBIT: Taxes = EBIT × effective tax rate. (Why this is OK — interest tax shield — "comes later in semester".)

## GAAP Statement of Cash Flows link
- GAAP CFO ≈ EBIT(1−T) + Dep − ΔNWC, but starts from net income = EBT(1−T).
- Fix: add after-tax interest: EBT(1−T) + I(1−T) = EBIT(1−T). Example EBT 10M, I 1M, T 21% → 7.9M vs 8.69M.
- Netflix example: GAAP CFO (2,877,322) + 599,132(1−.09) = (2,332,112); less net capex 387,064 → FCF = −2,719,176 ($000s).
